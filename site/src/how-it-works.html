How it works

What is in the widget, what it does for you, and how to put it in your app.

What's in the widget

A feedback board for Nimiq mini apps, in one tag. Your users report bugs, share ideas and write reviews. Everything is out in the open on the board: anyone can read it and vote. You answer under a post, set where it stands, and say which version fixed it.

  • Report a bug. Something does not work. A screenshot can come along.
  • Share an idea. Something your app is missing. Others vote for it.
  • Write a review. One to five stars and a few words, with the average on top. One per wallet.
  • Your answer. Planned, Done or Not doing, a line of your own, and "Fixed in" with a link to GitHub or GitLab. Whoever reported it gets a note.
  • Updates. A page of what changed in your app, newest first.

A screenshot only shows on the board once its author ticks that everyone may see it. Your app's version, if you pass it in, is for your eyes only.

Why app developers use it

  • Know what your users really want. Votes put the most wanted ideas and the most annoying bugs on top, so you know what to build next.
  • Fewer duplicates. While someone writes, similar posts show up under "Said already, maybe?", and they vote for that one instead.
  • One place to answer. Your reply, the status and the version that fixed it sit under the post, for everyone to see. No inbox, no email threads.
  • It looks like your app. Your colours and your words, in English, Dutch or German, and only the kinds of feedback you want. Try it in the configurator.
  • Your feedback, no sign-up. The posts live on a server you run, so nobody else holds them. Inside Nimiq Pay your users post with the wallet they already have: no account, no password.

How to add it

A server, a script and one tag. Paste it in, and your app has a feedback board.

Your appFeedback
Feedback
Feedback

  1. Start your server

    The board lives on a PleaseNoBugs server of your own. On Railway that is a button and three fields, no command line, about $1 a month; Node and Docker work too. When it starts, it prints your project id and its address.

    Set up a server

  2. Load the script

    <script src="https://unpkg.com/pleasenobugs@%VERSION%"></script>

    With a bundler: npm install pleasenobugs, then import 'pleasenobugs' once.

  3. Add the tag

    <please-no-bugs
  project="YOUR_PROJECT_ID"
  server="https://YOUR_SERVER"
  mode="floating"
></please-no-bugs>

    Use the project id and address your server printed.

  4. Pick how it shows

    mode="floating" Button
    A round button in the corner that opens the board. The one to start with.
    mode="inline" Inline
    The board inside a page of your own. Give it a height.
    mode="overlay" Overlay
    Over your page, straight away. Or open it from a menu item you already have:
    import { open } from 'pleasenobugs';
menuItem.onclick = () => open({ project, server });

    On a phone it covers the screen; on a wider screen it is a panel in the corner.

  5. Make it yours

    Pick your colours, words and kinds in the configurator. It writes this code for you, or a prompt for your AI agent.
